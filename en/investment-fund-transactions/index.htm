    Portfolio Diversification and Investment Funds

    Investment funds are investment instruments managed by professional fund managers that enable investors to invest their savings in money and capital market instruments. By purchasing investment fund units, investors gain exposure to portfolios comprising instruments such as equities, gold and other precious metals, and lease certificates (sukuk). Investors earn returns generated by these portfolios in proportion to their fund holdings.

    Investment funds may be established and managed by portfolio management companies authorized by the Capital Markets Board of Türkiye (CMB). Portfolio management companies may establish umbrella funds that bring together different investment instruments within a single portfolio. According to the classification established by the Capital Markets Board of Türkiye, the types of investment funds are as follows:

    Debt Instrument Funds

    Debt Instrument Funds are investment funds that continuously invest at least 80% of their total fund value in debt instruments. These instruments may include government debt securities, corporate debt securities, and a combination of both.

    Participation Funds

    Participation Funds are investment funds that invest in non-interest-bearing instruments approved by the Capital Markets Board of Türkiye (CMB), such as lease certificates (sukuk), gold and other precious metals, and equities. Compliant with Islamic finance principles, participation funds are particularly suitable for medium- and long-term investment strategies.

      Advantages of Investing in Investment Funds

      • Investment funds offer investors a wide range of benefits. The key advantages of investing in investment funds can be summarized as follows:

      • Investment funds are managed by professional portfolio management companies. Professional fund management provides investors with convenience and helps them save time.

      • Investment funds also offer advantages in terms of risk diversification. Professional fund managers diversify portfolio assets based on risk and return analyses. Diversifying a portfolio across securities with different characteristics enables more effective risk management.

      • Investment funds provide access to securities with high return potential that may otherwise be difficult to invest in with limited savings. Similarly, investors can easily access international investment instruments through funds that may be challenging to invest in individually.

      • Investment funds are also attractive due to their liquidity and ease of conversion into cash. Changes in fund values are reflected daily, and investors can redeem their profits or their entire investments in accordance with the terms and conditions specified in the fund prospectus.

      • Investment funds may also offer withholding tax advantages. Withholding tax rates on investment funds range from 0% to 5%.

      • Investment fund transactions can be carried out quickly and conveniently. Through Körfez Menkul's digital trading channels, you can easily buy and sell investment funds anytime, anywhere.

      Key Considerations When Choosing an Investment Fund

      Making successful investment decisions requires taking informed steps. When selecting an investment fund, it is important to consider certain key factors. Here are the main points to keep in mind when choosing an investment fund:

      Assess Your Risk Tolerance

      Investment funds carry the risk of losing a portion of the invested principal due to fluctuations in the value of the assets held in their portfolios. Furthermore, the protection provided by the Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (SDIF) for eligible deposits does not apply to investment funds.

      The risk level of investment funds varies depending on their investment strategies and underlying assets. Therefore, portfolio management companies assess and classify the risk levels of funds to help investors make informed decisions.

      When selecting an investment fund, it is important to assess your risk tolerance and understand the risk factors associated with each fund.

      Compare Fund Performance

      Reviewing the historical performance of investment funds and comparing different funds are important steps in making informed investment decisions. Investors can access fund performance reports through the Public Disclosure Platform (KAP). However, it is important to remember that past performance does not guarantee future returns.

      Consider Fund Expense Ratios

      Expense ratios vary across investment funds and can have a direct impact on investment returns. Funds with higher expense ratios may generate lower net returns due to increased costs.

      Investors should also consider their investment horizon when evaluating expense ratios. Over the long term, even small differences in expense ratios can have a significant cumulative impact on returns. For short-term investments, fund expenses should be assessed alongside potential returns and other transaction-related costs.

        • What Are the Types of Investment Funds and Their Characteristics?

          Investment fund types include debt instrument funds, participation funds, equity funds, precious metals funds, mixed and variable funds, money market funds, and fund of funds.

          Debt instrument funds invest in government and corporate debt securities. Participation funds invest in non-interest-bearing instruments compliant with Islamic finance principles, such as lease certificates (sukuk) and gold. Equity funds invest in shares traded on Borsa İstanbul, while precious metals funds invest in gold and other precious metals.

          Mixed and variable funds combine various capital market instruments to diversify risk. Money market funds invest in short-term, relatively low-risk instruments and are generally suitable for short-term investment strategies. Fund of funds provide diversification by investing in portfolios comprising different investment funds.

        • How Do Fees and Taxation Work for Investment Funds?

          Gains realized from the sale of investment fund units are subject to withholding tax, with applicable rates varying depending on the investment holding period. A 5% withholding tax applies to gains from funds held for less than six months, while a 3% withholding tax applies to gains from funds held for less than one year. Gains from funds held for more than one year are exempt from withholding tax.

          Although corporate investors may be exempt from withholding tax, they remain subject to corporate income tax. Management fees and other expenses that may affect fund returns are disclosed in the respective fund prospectuses.
