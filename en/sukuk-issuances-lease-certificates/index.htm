    Sukuk Issuance: Company Growth with Innovative Financial Instruments

    Sukuk, also known as lease certificates, are Islamic financial instruments issued by an Asset Leasing Company (ALC). They grant the right to ownership and to receive dividends or lease income from an asset.

     Investors in Sukuk earn fixed or variable returns at regular intervals from the income of this asset, proportionate to their shares. Offering interest-free earnings, Sukuk are highly liquid instruments traded in secondary markets. Especially attractive to qualified investors, Sukuk can provide financing for both public and private sector companies.

    Körfez Menkul issues Sukuk by transferring the certificates of assets in its portfolio to investors through an Asset Leasing Company. By investing in Sukuk, you can earn regular returns from the assets you have a stake in, proportionate to your share.

    Structure and Operation of Sukuk (Lease Certificates)

    The issuance of lease certificates is a widely adopted financing method globally. Defined as asset-backed financial certificates, the operation of lease certificates occurs through the issuance of Sukuk by authorized institutions. Companies issuing Sukuk must share their activities transparently with the public. To invest in Sukuk, an agreement adhering to Islamic finance principles is signed between the Asset Leasing Company and the investor. Sukuk types can be broadly categorized into five based on the type of contract, issuance purpose, and financing characteristics.

    Management Agreement-Based Lease Certificate / Wakala Sukuk

    Management agreement-based lease certificates rely on managing assets or rights in favor of the ALC, including leasing them for a specific period. Known as Wakala Sukuk, the income from the underlying asset portfolio obtained within the management agreement framework is transferred to investors.

    Ownership-Based Lease Certificate / Ijara Sukuk

    Ijara Sukuk can be defined as financial instruments representing shares in the ownership and rental income of leased real estate. Ownership-based lease certificates can be issued to finance assets and rights, leased to third parties or the resource organization, or managed on behalf of the asset organization.

    Purchase and Sale-Based Lease Certificate / Murabaha Sukuk

    Murabaha Sukuk involves the purchase of an asset or right under certain conditions and then its sale on credit to companies with specific characteristics. Investors have a receivable right through purchase and sale-based lease certificates.

    Partnership-Based Lease Certificate / Mudaraba & Musharaka Sukuk

    Partnership-based lease certificates are divided into Mudaraba Sukuk and Musharaka Sukuk. Mudaraba Sukuk is an investment certificate with equal shares, indicating ownership rights divided into equal shares over an asset. Musharaka Sukuk is based on a partnership agreement, representing participants' capital contributions in joint ventures. These types of partnership-based Sukuk are especially used for financing large-scale investments.

    Project Agreement-Based Lease Certificate / Istisna Sukuk

    Istisna Sukuk are financial instruments issued to provide financing for the production of products within the scope of the certificate. The ALC can lease the project or directly subject it to a sales contract. Investors obtain ownership of the products in question through these certificates. This type of Sukuk, operating within a project agreement framework, is preferred as a financing source for major infrastructure projects.
