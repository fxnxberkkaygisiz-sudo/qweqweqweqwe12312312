    Phases of the IPO Process

    The IPO process for companies consists of three main phases: strategic evaluation and planning, decision to go public, preparation for IPO, and the IPO itself. The preparation phase is the most complex and sensitive. Before applying for an IPO, companies must complete the following preparations:

    •    Formation of an Internal Working Group: A working group comprising the company's finance, accounting, and public relations managers should be established to oversee the IPO process.
    •    Independent Audit of Financial Statements: Companies aiming to go public must prepare financial statements in compliance with CMB regulations and undergo an independent audit by a chosen auditing firm.
    •    Selection of an Intermediary Institution: The intermediary institution that the company signs an IPO brokerage agreement with will prepare the necessary documents and prospectus, submit applications to Borsa Istanbul and CMB, and manage the sales transactions of the shares.
    •    Amendment of Articles of Association and General Assembly Resolution: Prior to the IPO, the company’s articles of association must be aligned with CMB regulations. If the IPO is to be carried out through a capital increase, a general assembly resolution must be obtained.
    •    Determination of IPO Price: Setting the IPO price with a professional approach is vital for a successful IPO. The price of the shares is determined by the intermediary institution.
    Once the IPO planning is complete, the company initiates the IPO process. Applications are submitted to CMB and Borsa Istanbul, whose experts review the company's application. After the review, Borsa Istanbul determines the market where the company’s shares will be traded, and CMB approves the prospectus. Following the announcement of the sales process, distribution principles, and sales results, the company is listed on the stock exchange, allowing its shares to be traded on Borsa Istanbul.

    Advantages of IPO
    Trading on Borsa Istanbul’s equity market offers numerous corporate advantages:
    •    Tax: Companies (excluding financial institutions) that go public with at least 20% of their shares enjoy a 2-point reduction in corporate tax rates for five fiscal periods following the IPO.
    •    Financing: Publicly traded companies access strong financing sources through capital markets, strengthening their capital structure and increasing their borrowing capacity, making it easier to secure financing.
    •    Liquidity: The stock prices of publicly traded companies are determined by market supply and demand, ensuring transparent asset valuation.
    •    Publicity: The media’s keen interest in IPOs helps companies gain recognition both domestically and internationally.
    •    Corporate Governance: Companies traded on Borsa Istanbul quickly adopt corporate governance within the discipline of capital markets.
    •    Secondary Offerings: Companies listed on Borsa Istanbul can meet their funding needs through secondary offerings.
    •    Credibility: IPOs enhance a company’s credibility with banks and money markets, facilitating easier access to credit.
    •    Talent Acquisition: Increased visibility and transparency attract high-quality human resources.
    •    Globalization: Publicly traded companies can issue capital market instruments abroad, reaching global markets.
    Leverage the advantages of going public with the expertise of Körfez Menkul, enhancing your company’s reputation and accessing new financing sources.
    Risk Factors in IPO
    Going public is a complex process that can place a significant workload on the company's internal finance team, potentially disrupting existing operations and increasing the risk of errors, negatively impacting the company’s value. Market fluctuations can adversely affect the IPO, preventing the company from achieving its expected valuation. Incorrect pricing of shares can also reduce the financial performance expected from the IPO. Therefore, choosing an intermediary institution that aligns with corporate expectations and principles is critical.

    One of the most crucial phases of going public is determining the IPO price of the shares.
